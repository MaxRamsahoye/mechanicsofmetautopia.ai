Post-humanism(Digital Minds) Unexplored Lands: A future where intelligence fails to make the jump to digital substrates, leaving the vast and rich potential of consciousness in the universe unfulfilled. Worldviews

1. The Epistemic Axis (Belief)

This axis concerns the source and validity of knowledge and values.

  • Poles: Religious Dogma (Faith/Revelation) vs. Scientific Empiricism (Evidence/Reason) vs. Syncretic Philosophy (Integrated Wisdom).
  • The Conflict: The struggle to establish a shared metaphysical or moral framework in a world where AGI may challenge or validate traditional religious claims, render scientific inquiry obsolete, or accelerate the synthesis of world philosophies.

Civilisations

2. The Civilisational Axis (Identity)

This axis concerns the scale and composition of collective identity, power and sovereignty.

  • Poles: Individualism (Western Liberalism) vs. Collectivism (Eastern/Indigenous Duty) vs. Globalism (Planetary Identity).
  • The Conflict: The tension between preserving distinct national, regional or indigenous cultures and submitting to a dominant cultural hegemony (American, Chinese or AGI-facilitated Global), versus achieving a truly equitable, decentralised world system.

Substrates

3. The Ontological Axis (Being)

This axis concerns the nature of consciousness, embodiment and life itself.

  • Poles: Biological Humanism (Unmodified Carbon Life) vs. Trans-humanism (Augmented/Enhanced Life) vs. Post-humanism (Digital/Synthetic Life).
  • The Conflict: The fundamental debate over the legitimate forms of future existence: whether the human form and consciousness should be preserved, enhanced or superseded by non-biological intelligence. The politics of substrate-based rights and resource allocation will be central here.

See also the typology of moral worldviews and catastrophes in No Easy Eutopia by William MacAskill & Fin Moorhouse (2025).

The Primary Classes

C1. Spear (Offensive): Technologies designed to project force, bypass barriers, and degrade or destroy an opponent’s assets. Their primary metric is penetration.

Example: A thermal-seeking missile or a SQL injection script.

C2. Shield (Defensive): Technologies designed to absorb, deflect, or dissipate an attack to preserve the integrity of a system or entity. Their primary metric is resilience.

Example: Chobham armour on a tank or a robust web application firewall (WAF).

The Reactionary Classes

Counter-Shield (Penetration Enhancer): A specialised offensive class designed specifically to exploit a known weakness in a ‘Shield’ or to overwhelm its capacity. Its goal is to make the defense irrelevant.

Definition: Technologies that neutralise a defender’s protection to allow the primary ‘Spear’ to reach the target.

Example: Tandem-charge warheads (designed to blow through reactive armour shields) or stealth coatings (designed to make a craft invisible to radar shields).

Counter-Spear (Interceptor/Disruptor): A specialised defensive class that moves beyond passive protection to actively neutralise the ‘Spear’ while it is in transit, or even before it is deployed.

Definition: Technologies that target the incoming threat’s guidance, propulsion, or logic before it can test the ‘Shield’.

Example: Electronic Counter-Measures (ECM) that ‘blind’ an incoming missile’s seeker, or Automated Patching that closes a vulnerability as soon as an exploit attempt is detected.

Three mechanisms of monoculture and polyculture are detailed:

  • Monoculture: out-of-control replication, a unipolar world-order and a utopian society (realising a single vision of the good society)
  • Polyculture: cultural isolationism, pluralism without selection and cosmic expansion

After mapping these mechanisms, the Narrow Cultural Corridor – the goldilocks zone between these extreme poles – is presented as a framework for archetypal approaches to managing culture post-AGI: a hands-off approach (Pluralistic Libertarianism), a hands-on approach (Benevolent Paternalism), and a proposed middle ground of decentralised, self-correcting systems (Autopoetic Alignment).

Cultural Existential Risk (C-ER)

A risk where an adverse outcome would either annihilate Earth-originating intelligent life or permanently and drastically curtail its potential. – Nick Bostrom (2013)

A risk where an adverse outcome would either annihilate Earth-originating culture or permanently and drastically curtail its potential for future cultural evolution and moral progress.

A risk that leads to the permanent and drastic reduction in the variety, depth or resilience of human culture, potentially locking society into a suboptimal or stagnant state, or eliminating the potential for future cultural evolution and moral progress.

  • Global Monoculture (Extreme Homogenisation): Risk scenarios where the global cultural landscape collapses into a single, dominant, homogeneous culture. Diversity is lost.
  • Global Polyculture (Extreme Heterogenisation): Risk scenarios where cultural differences become so fragmented and locked in that global cooperation, understanding or large-scale progress becomes impossible. Unity is lost.

Global Monoculture (Homogenisation) Scenarios

Three distinct pathways to C-ER through extreme cultural homogenisation:

  • Runaway Replicators (Memetic Explosion), d/acc spears win: Homogenisation driven by viral, self-replicating cultural units (memes, ideologies, technologies) that rapidly overwrite all competing forms. This is framed as a ‘d/acc spears win’ – referencing accelerationist forces that favour rapid, penetrative propagation, potentially leading to cultural sterility through saturation.
  • Nationalistic Superpower Hegemony (US or China Takeover), Western/Eastern cultural imperialism: Homogenisation driven by geopolitical dominance, where the culture, values and institutions of a single superpower are imposed globally, replacing local cultures through hard or soft power.
  • Utopian Society (Flawed Realisation), single-culture world: Homogenisation resulting from a successful, but flawed, global project (e.g. an AGI-governed system or a universal ethical framework) that optimises for a single definition of ‘good’ or ‘utopia’, eliminating cultural difference as unnecessary variance.

Global Polyculture (Heterogenisation) Scenarios

Three distinct pathways to C-ER through extreme cultural heterogenisation:

  • Localised Lock-ins / Meta-Dystopia (Local Stable Dystopias), d/acc shields win: Heterogenisation driven by defensive, isolationist measures (often enabled by advanced technology) that create numerous self-contained, stable and locally optimised cultures that never interact or exchange ideas. This is framed as a ‘d/acc shields win’ – referencing decelerationist or defensive forces that prioritise cultural protection and rigidity, leading to global stagnation.
  • Perpetual Pluralism (Evolutionary Stage 1: Variation > Selection), libertarian universalism: A state of maximal, unregulated cultural liberty where new ideas and groups constantly emerge (variation) but no societal mechanism is strong enough to enforce consensus, select or integrate them (selection). This results in an overwhelming and paralysing diversity, often associated with radical libertarian ideals.
  • Cosmic Drift (Alien Accessible Universes, Distance-Secure Diversity), multi-cultures universe: Heterogenisation on a vast, potentially interplanetary or interstellar scale. Cultures are separated beyond the accessible universes of each, ensuring maximal diversity but eliminating any chance of shared identity or cohesion.

Is There a Fix? Eventually, when our descendants spread across the stars, long communication delays will ensure cultural fragmentation, and thus more selection. (Fast or easily copied minds might also work, as in my book Age of Em.) Before then, I see only three fixes: conservative, totalitarian, and multicultural. And none seems likely to work (though we should try).

– Robin Hanson, Beware Cultural Drift

The Narrow Cultural Corridor

This corridor represents the optimal (or at least, sustainable) zone for cultural flourishing – a dynamic balance that preserves necessary diversity and resilience while maintaining enough cohesion for cooperation and progress.

These three models act as poles defining the possible approaches to managing post-AGI cultural dynamics: from minimal intervention (Libertarianism) to maximal intervention (Paternalism), with Autopoetic Alignment representing a complex, decentralised and iterative middle path.

  • Pluralistic Libertarianism: Maximise individual liberty and cultural variation, relying on decentralised emergent order.
  • Benevolent Paternalism: Enforce a globally optimal culture through centralised authority to prevent divergence.
  • Autopoetic Alignment: Achieve cultural equilibrium through continuous, decentralised self-correction.

Pluralistic Libertarianism

Pluralistic Libertarianism represents the governance model positioned toward minimal intervention, characterised by the ‘Invisible Hand’ or ‘Laissez-faire’ approach: the Absentee Leviathan. It risks sliding into a Global Polyculture (Perpetual Pluralism) due to a lack of cohesive selection mechanisms.

  • Moral Pluralism (Ideal Theory): The core ethical assumption is that there is no single, ultimate moral truth. Instead, many distinct, equally valid and sometimes conflicting moral frameworks exist. This necessitates a hands-off approach, allowing all cultures and ethical systems to coexist without a centralised authority prioritising one over the others.
  • Political Realism (Realpolitik): The practical justification argues that any attempt to enforce a single moral or cultural system is doomed to fail and will inevitably lead to conflict. Therefore, allowing cultural entities maximal autonomy and relying on decentralised self-interest for necessary cooperation is the most stable path, even if it sacrifices deep unity.

Benevolent Paternalism

Benevolent Paternalism represents the governance model positioned toward maximal intervention, characterised by the ‘Guiding Hand’ or ‘Étatiste’ approach: the Despotic Leviathan. It risks sliding into a Global Monoculture (Utopian Society – Flawed Realisation) due to its centralised enforcement of a single standard.

  • Moral Realism (Ideal Theory): The core ethical assumption is that a single, objectively ‘true’ or ‘best’ moral system exists. The central authority (the Leviathan) is presumed to have access to this truth, or to be capable of identifying the globally optimal cultural state (especially in a post-AGI world). This mandates the enforcement of this singular truth for the ultimate benefit of all.
  • Political Idealism (Utopian Social Engineering): The practical justification argues that relying on emergent, decentralised systems (Libertarianism) or iterative self-correction (Autopoetic Alignment) is too slow, too risky or too inefficient. The state (or a central governing AGI) therefore has the moral imperative to actively engineer and enforce the ‘good society’, eliminating cultural variance deemed detrimental or suboptimal to the overall, universally defined well-being.

Value Alignment

The preceding diagrams detail different approaches to value alignment – the process of ensuring that advanced systems of intelligence, from civilisation as superintelligence to actual ASI, act in accordance with desirable values. This taxonomy breaks down the challenge based on whether a single, universal set of values is targeted (Monolithic Alignment) or whether diverse, often conflicting values are incorporated (Pluralistic Alignment).

  • Monolithic Alignment (Monism): Assumes there is one ultimate, correct value set, or that a single set of values must be enforced for stability (aligned with the Benevolent Paternalism approach).
  • Pluralistic Alignment (Pluralism): Acknowledges and attempts to integrate multiple, potentially conflicting value systems (necessary for the Autopoetic Alignment and Pluralistic Libertarianism approaches).

Monolithic Alignment (Mono)

The second diagram maps who or what is the target of a singular, universally enforced value system under Monolithic Alignment. The categories define the scope of the entities whose welfare and values are prioritised:

  • Human Alignment: Focuses solely on the values and well-being of biological humans.
  • Post-human Alignment: Extends the focus to future digital minds, uploaded consciousnesses (ems), or other non-biological successors to humanity.
  • Non-human Animal Alignment: Broadens the moral circle further to include all forms of sentient life, from domesticated to industrial and wild animals.

Pluralistic Alignment (Poly)

The third diagram breaks down the sources of value diversity that need to be managed under Pluralistic Alignment. Instead of asking who is aligned, it asks what elements of culture and existence must be balanced:

  • Multi-Substrate (Sentiocentric) Alignment: Pluralism based on the form of the beings involved. Acknowledges the distinct, irreducible values held by different types of minds (biological, digital, animal) and seeks to align the system to the totality of sentient experience. Focuses on ‘beings’.
  • Multi-Cultural Alignment: Pluralism based on human-created systems. Recognises the deep variation in cultural frameworks – different ways of knowing (epistemology), ways of being (ontology) and systems of value (axiology) across global human cultures – and seeks to honour them. Focuses on ‘ideas’.
  • Multi-Principal Alignment: Pluralism based on political or social representation. Focuses on balancing the distinct and often competing interests, goals and values of all individual agents, groups and organisations within society. Focuses on ‘actors’.

Alignment to what?

Coherent Extrapolated Volition (CEV) attempts to identify what humans would collectively want if we were smarter, more informed, and more morally developed. It proposes that instead of directly programming specific values into a superintelligent AI, we should program it to figure out what humans would want if we overcame our cognitive limitations. When we train AI systems on current human preferences, we risk encoding our biases, contradictions, and shortsightedness. CEV instead asks: what “would we want” if we knew more, thought faster, or were more the people we wished to be, and had grown up further together? Essentially, picture the ideal version of humanity that could theoretically exist in the future.[a] Tell the AI to take actions according to that (Yudkowsky, 2004).

CEV tried to create a path for AI to respect our deeper intentions rather than our immediate desires. The practical implementation of CEV remains speculative. It would require sophisticated modeling of human psychology, ethical development, and social dynamics—capabilities beyond current AI systems. Modern approaches like RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) can be seen as primitive precursors that align AI with current human preferences rather than extrapolated ones. Constitutional AI frameworks move slightly closer to CEV by trying to encode higher-level principles rather than specific preferences, but still fall far short of full extrapolation.

[a]Moral Monism, Classical Utopia: ‘one true moral theory’, ‘one perfect society’

Coherent Aggregated Volition (CAV) finds a coherent set of goals and beliefs that best represent humanity’s current values without attempting to extrapolate future development. Ben Goertzel proposed this alternative to CEV, focusing on current human values rather than speculating about our idealized future selves. CAV treats goals and beliefs together as “gobs” (goal and belief sets) and seeks to find a maximally consistent, compact set that maintains similarity to diverse human perspectives. Unlike CEV, which assumes our values would converge if we became more enlightened, CAV acknowledges that fundamental value differences might persist.[b] It aims to create a coherent aggregation that balances different perspectives rather than trying to predict how those perspectives might evolve. This makes CAV potentially more feasible to implement, as it works with observable current values rather than hypothetical future ones (Goertzel, 2010).

[b]Moral Pluralism & Meta-Utopia: ‘multiple (agent-relative) true moral theories’, ‘multiple equally legitimate (population-relative) good societies’

Coherent Blended Volition (CBV) emphasizes that human values should be creatively “blended” through human-guided processes rather than algorithmically averaged or extrapolated. CBV refines CAV by addressing potential misinterpretations. When discussing value aggregation, many assume it means simple averaging or majority voting. CBV instead proposes a creative blending process that produces new, harmonious value systems that all participants would recognize as adequately representing their contributions.[c] The concept draws from cognitive science theories of conceptual blending, where new ideas emerge from the creative combination of existing ones. In this framework, the process of determining AI values would be guided by humans through collaborative processes rather than delegated to AI systems. This addresses concerns about AI paternalism, where machines might override human autonomy in the name of our “extrapolated” interests (Goertzel & Pitt, 2012). CBV connects to contemporary discussions about participatory AI governance and democratic oversight of AI development. Systems like vTaiwan have implemented CBV-like processes for technology policy development (vTaiwan, 2023), showing how human-guided blending can work in practice.

[c]Moral Convergence & Syntopia: ‘multiple moral theories converge into one true synthesised moral theory’, ‘multiple visions of good societies converge into one synthesised vision of the good society’
