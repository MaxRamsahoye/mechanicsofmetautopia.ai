Simulator

How one sub-topia takes over another in the cellular automaton of The Mechanics of Meta-Utopia: a culture crosses a border when its cultural integrity overcomes the neighbour’s membrane impermeability, and then the neighbour’s own cultural integrity.

  1. Each hexagon is a sub-topia. Its fill is its culture; the number inside is its cultural integrity; the label beneath is its sub-topian status; its outline is its membrane, thicker and darker the more impermeable it is.

  2. A’s culture reaches B’s border. It crosses only if A’s cultural integrity (8.2) is higher than B’s membrane impermeability (5.1). It is: the membrane gives way.

  3. Inside, A’s culture meets B’s. It takes hold only if A’s cultural integrity (8.2) is also higher than B’s (3.5). It is: B cannot hold on to its own culture.

  4. B adopts A’s culture, and with it A’s sub-topian status, cultural integrity and membrane: B is now part of The Culture, utopian, with an integrity of 8.2 and a membrane of 4.0.

  5. Two sub-topias, one culture. On a full map the same two tests run between every pair of neighbours, turn after turn, until nothing more changes.

The rule

Every sub-topia carries four things: a culture; a sub-topian status, whether it is utopian, notopian or dystopian; a cultural integrity, how strongly that culture holds and pulls; and a membrane impermeability, how closed its borders are. Only integrity and membrane decide whether a culture crosses, and its status travels with it. A culture crosses from one sub-topia into its neighbour only by passing two tests, in order. The tests run in both directions at once: A against B, and B against A.

Test 1

The membrane

integrityA > membraneB

Is the incoming culture’s cultural integrity higher than the neighbour’s membrane impermeability? If not, it is stopped at the border.

Test 2

The culture inside

integrityA > integrityB

Is it also higher than the neighbour’s own cultural integrity? If not, it gets in but cannot take hold.

Above, A’s integrity of 8.2 beats B’s membrane of 5.1, and then B’s integrity of 3.5, while B’s integrity of 3.5 cannot get through A’s membrane of 4.0. B is assimilated: it takes on A’s culture together with A’s status, integrity and membrane, and turns from dystopian to utopian.

Three outcomes

Keep A as it is above, with a cultural integrity of 8.2 and a membrane of 4.0, and change its neighbour. Whether A is blocked, deflected or assimilates B depends on B’s two numbers: its membrane impermeability, then its own cultural integrity. Each card also tests B against A: a B with an integrity above 8.2 would pass both tests and take over A instead. In each case, the number in a hexagon is its cultural integrity.

From two sub-topias to a civilisation

The same two tests run between every pair of neighbours on a map, all at once, turn after turn, until no more cultures change. Where membranes are high everywhere, cultures stay sealed in place: enclosure. Where membranes are low and one culture’s integrity is far above the rest, it spreads from neighbour to neighbour: takeover. The typology of the four attractor states describes where each combination leads.
