Simulator

How one sub-topia takes over another in the cellular automaton of The Mechanics of Meta-Utopia: a culture crosses a border when its cultural integrity overcomes the neighbour’s membrane impermeability, and then the neighbour’s own cultural integrity.

The rule

Every sub-topia carries three things: a culture, a cultural integrity (how strongly its culture holds and pulls) and a membrane impermeability (how closed its borders are). When two sub-topias are neighbours, a culture crosses from one to the other only by passing two tests, in order:

  1. The membrane. Is the incoming culture’s cultural integrity higher than the neighbour’s membrane impermeability? If not, it is stopped at the border.
  2. The culture inside. Is it also higher than the neighbour’s own cultural integrity? If not, it gets in but cannot take hold.

Here, A’s integrity of 8.2 beats B’s membrane of 5.1, and then B’s integrity of 3.5: B is assimilated, and takes on A’s culture together with A’s integrity and membrane.

Three outcomes

OutcomeWhenWhat happens
BlockedIntegrity ≤ the neighbour’s membraneThe culture stops at the border; nothing changes.
DeflectedIntegrity > the membrane, but ≤ the neighbour’s integrityThe culture crosses the border but cannot displace the one inside; nothing changes.
AssimilatedIntegrity > the membrane and > the neighbour’s integrityThe neighbour takes on the incoming culture, with its integrity and membrane.

From two sub-topias to a civilisation

The same two tests run between every pair of neighbours on a map, all at once, turn after turn, until no more cultures change. Where membranes are high everywhere, cultures stay sealed in place: enclosure. Where membranes are low and one culture’s integrity is far above the rest, it spreads from neighbour to neighbour: takeover. The typology of the four attractor states describes where each combination leads.
