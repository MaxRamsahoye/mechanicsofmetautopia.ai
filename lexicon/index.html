Curated Terms

Meta-Utopia
Nozick’s ideal world-society according to libertarian political philosophy, which proposes that ‘utopia will consist of utopias, of many different and divergent communities’.
Sub-topias
The communities within a meta-utopia – Hanson’s ‘gardens’ – each with its own culture and way of life.
Boundaries
The ‘walls’ between sub-topias, in Hanson’s terms: the physical, legal and digital borders through which people and ideas pass, or are kept out.
Civilisation Design
The design of a world-system as a sociotechnical architecture: here, through its two variables, cultural integrity and membrane permeability, to steer towards desirable civilisational equilibria.
Cultural Integrity
The persistence of a sub-topia’s collective cultural identity across time, through inter-topian immigration and inter-generational transmission: how well it maintains cultural continuity and resists cultural change, the strength of its memetic immune system.
Membrane Permeability
The degree to which the boundaries between sub-topias are open or closed to novel information from digital communication or physical migration, set by their border control and migration policy: how easily citizens can move to another community, or a meme spread across sub-topias.
Four Futures
A typology of post-AGI cultural attractor states, from maximal and minimal levels of cultural integrity and membrane permeability: a desired multi-cultural equilibrium threatened by three cultural existential risks.
Meta-Utopian Uplift
The transition to a semi-compartmentalised world-system with boundaries open enough for multi-culturalism, freedom of movement and moral progress, but sufficiently protected to prevent hostile cultural takeover: epistemic equilibria.
Mono-Cultural Takeover
An expansionist culture or an invasive memetic replicator breaks out of one sub-topia and colonises all others, proliferating to the point of extreme cultural homogeneity across the entire civilisation: gray goo.
Meta-Dystopian Enclosure
A defense-dominant, maximally decentralised world-system where every sub-topia is a completely closed system, with memetic transmission and physical migration rendered functionally impossible: localised lock-ins.
Cultural Collapse
A null state where all sub-topias have lost their coherent, collective, cultural identity and localised lock-downs prevent the creation of new sub-topias, leading to civilisational stagnation by default: a permanent plateau.
Clash of Civilisations
An underdetermined, hybrid condition – exhibiting proto-characteristics of mono-culture, meta-dystopia and cultural collapse – where multiple cultures compete for survival, sovereignty and supremacy. After Huntington: the condition of world politics since the end of the Cold War.
Narrow Cultural Corridor
The path the governance of cultural evolution must navigate between pluralistic libertarianism and benevolent paternalism, both of which lead to moral stagnation and the flawed realisation of civilisation’s potential.
Pluralistic Libertarianism
A minimalist, laissez-faire approach to the governance of cultural evolution, which maximises individual liberty and cultural variation and relies on decentralised emergent order – a global poly-culture that persists without progress.
Benevolent Paternalism
A maximalist, interventionist approach to the governance of cultural evolution, which enforces a globally optimal culture through centralised authority – a global monoculture that ends multi-cultural diversity.
Autopoetic Alignment
The middle path between pluralistic libertarianism and benevolent paternalism: sustaining multi-cultural equilibria through continuous, holarchical self-correction.
Symmetry Problem
The core design problem: mechanisms that mitigate against monocultural takeover risk producing lock-ins, and mechanisms that mitigate against lock-ins risk enabling monocultural takeover.
Memetic Shields
Defensive technologies that protect a community’s cultural integrity by limiting exposure to external influence: closing borders, filtering information, controlling what members encounter from outside.
Stadial Transition
Cultural transition viewed in stages: from the current world-system, both good and bad, to a stable viatopia, then metautopia and, potentially, syntopia.
Viatopia
MacAskill’s frame for the transition to a post-AGI society: a stable state of minimum necessary coordination to achieve existential security, ‘where many different moral points of view can flourish’.
Metautopia
The second stage of cultural transition: an equilibrium between political and cultural groups, and a more realistic and desirable vision than full synthesis.
Syntopia
An ideal civilisational endstate in which humanity has converged on a universal morality, ‘perennial philosophy’ and syncretic culture, transcending and including all worldviews from history.

Coherent Blended Volition
A creative blending process that produces new, harmonious value systems that all participants would recognise as adequately representing their contributions.
Cultural Collapse

Cultural Existential Risk
A threat to the desired multi-cultural equilibrium of meta-utopia. The article names three: mono-cultural takeover, meta-dystopian enclosure and cultural collapse.

Distance-Secure Diversity
Diversity by default, from physical distance: isolation from outsiders, by mountain ranges, remote islands or interstellar space, that lets local value lock-ins persist for thousands of years.
Evolutionary Stable Strategy
A transitional strategy resistant to invading strategies that would default to monocultural takeover or meta-dystopian lock-in, so that maintaining the meta-utopian structure is every sub-state’s best local strategy.
Four Futures

Meta-Utopian Enclosure
A subtle meta-dystopia: every sub-topia is a sub-utopia and everyone is in the one they want, yet no one can move to another, through hyper-defensive governance against memetic competition.

Narrow Cultural Corridor

Perpetual Pluralism
A lesser moral catastrophe: cultural variation persisting indefinitely, with no reflective selection process to refine the set of sub-utopias towards the most morally advanced.
Pluralistic Libertarianism
A minimalist, laissez-faire approach to the governance of cultural evolution, which maximises individual liberty and cultural variation and relies on decentralised emergent order – a global poly-culture that persists without progress.
Recoverable Collapse
Collapse that is partial or localised, so that the capacity for cultural pattern maintenance survives and a later stimulus can bring a new renaissance.
Recurrent Collapse
A cyclical condition in which civilisation repeatedly achieves a level of cultural progress, only to collapse before achieving stability: an eternal return.
Runaway Replicators
Monocultural takeover by selection: an adaptive memeplex proliferates to the point of extreme cultural homogeneity, the memetic equivalent in mind-space of a ‘grey goo’ scenario.
Soft Superpower
Monocultural takeover by force: a single nation-state wins the arms race in AI and uses its decisive advantage to secure control of the world-system, its belief and value-systems taking over.
Stadial Transition
Cultural transition viewed in stages: from the current world-system, both good and bad, to a stable viatopia, then metautopia and, potentially, syntopia.
Sub-topias

Symmetry Problem

Syntopia
An ideal civilisational endstate in which humanity has converged on a universal morality, ‘perennial philosophy’ and syncretic culture, transcending and including all worldviews from history.
Unidimensional Utopia
Monocultural takeover by hyperstition: a single utopian vision of the good society becomes universally adopted and brings itself into existence, a flawed realisation of civilisation’s cultural potential.
Unrecoverable Collapse
A permanent null state where the institutions of cultural pattern maintenance fail so completely that the knowledge, values or ambition for cultural experimentation are lost, and enclosure keeps out any restorative influence.
Viatopia
MacAskill’s frame for the transition to a post-AGI society: a stable state of minimum necessary coordination to achieve existential security, ‘where many different moral points of view can flourish’.
