How the model works

The grid is divided into twelve sub-topias, each starting with its own home culture. At every step, a community meets one of its neighbours, and one of a few things can happen:

  • Transmission. A culture passes from one community to the next: within a sub-topia, its own culture at the rate of its cultural integrity; across a border, or a foreign culture inside it, at the rate of its membrane permeability.
  • Resistance. A community holding its sub-topia’s home culture resists a foreign one as strongly as its integrity, and draws lapsed or converted neighbours back to it.
  • Lapse. Without integrity, a community loses its culture and turns grey, until something refills it.
  • Competition. When cultures meet, the fitter tends to win; one culture is expansionist, and wins a little more often.
  • Synthesis. Where integrity and permeability are both high, cultures meeting at a border sometimes blend into a new one.

High integrity with closed borders seals each sub-topia (enclosure); with open borders, cultures trade and blend while each sub-topia keeps its own (uplift). Low integrity with open borders lets the expansionist culture take the map (takeover); with closed borders, cultures simply fade (collapse). In between, they compete (the clash).

The Unified Frames and the descriptions of each state are in the typology of the four attractor states.
