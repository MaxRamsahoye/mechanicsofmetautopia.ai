The Political Philosophy Turn in AI Governance & Macrostrategy Research

AI alignment, once the subject of science-fiction and then machine learning after that, is now entering the province of political philosophy. As an emerging discourse on an emerging technology, one might ask where we are to begin? The libertarian political philosopher Robert Nozick provides a definitive answer: “Political philosophers now must either work within [John] Rawls’ theory [of justice] or explain why not.”

It was this latter task that Nozick’s 1974 magnum opus Anarchy, State and Utopia set out to undertake in response to Rawls’s 1971 A Theory of Justice. Over 50 years on and these works are considered the canon of modern political and social thought. However, as products of their time in history, neither mentions ‘artificial intelligence’ once. There is thus a need to re-read Rawlsianism and Nozickianism through the lens of AI and vice versa, to read the 21st century technology of AI through 20th century political philosophy. And by doing so, to pioneer a new political philosophy of AI.

This is one of the principal purposes of this research project on post-AGI civilisation design. However we are not the first to chart this intersection. Before us, following in Rawls’s and Nozick’s footsteps are Iason Gabriel – chief research scientist, ethicist and post-AGI researcher at the Big Tech lab Google DeepMind – and William MacAskill – founder, director and senior research fellow of the macrostrategy institute Forethought.

Gabriel is our contemporary Rawls, authoring a paper in his honour Towards a Theory of Justice for AI (2022) as well as further papers Using the Veil of Ignorance to align AI systems with principles of justice (2023), A matter of principle? AI alignment as the fair treatment of claims (2025) & Artificial intelligence, values, and alignment (2020) which all bring Rawls’s political philosophy to bear on the AI discourse. The crux for Gabriel is that alignment should not be a single-shot solution to a monolithic formula (‘human values’), but should instead be procedural and pluralistic:

❝ the central challenge for [political and AI] theorists is not to identify ‘true’ moral principles for AI; rather, it is to identify fair principles for alignment that receive reflective endorsement despite widespread variation in people’s moral beliefs. ❞

MacAskill is our Nozick in the ‘Age of AGI’, proposing that we navigate ‘the transition to post-AGI society’ with the north star of Viatopia:

❝ a state of society where existential risk is very low, where many different moral points of view can flourish, where many possible futures are still open to us, and where major decisions are made via thoughtful, reflective processes. ❞

Genealogically, MacAskill’s Viatopia is a higher-order conceptual engineering incorporating Nozick’s meta-utopia (‘a framework for utopias, a place where people… pursue their own vision of the good life’) as one desideratum (‘many different moral points of view can flourish’) for an intermediate stage of civilisation before undergoing a period of Long Reflection. This makes MacAskill, or more so Ord, another successor of Rawls’s – in his case the notion of ‘reflective equilibrium’ – who coined the Long Reflection in The Precipice (2020) as a “time to reflect upon what we truly desire; upon which of these visions for humanity would be the best realization of our potential”.

Viatopia & The Saturation View

When I first framed the project, it was in part a commentary on Viatopia. Since then, MacAskill released an essay presenting The Saturation View (April, 2026) in population ethics where he identifies ‘the monoculture problem’ which lines up with the risk of ‘monocultural takeover’ as I phrase it in MoMU as a parallel to AI takeover and human takeover, emphasising the cultural dimension over the political.

The Saturation View vs Mechanics of Meta-Utopia

MacAskill is thinking more in terms of maximally hedonic digital minds as an ideal monoculture versus monoculture in biohuman civilisation which is what MoMU pertains to. MoMU 1.0 doesn’t talk as much about AI minds and ‘stratified utopia’ (post-human, post-planetary meta-utopia), but further research should.

Homage to Nozick

In a footnote and during a podcast with Liv Boeree, MacAskill references Nozick’s ‘meta-utopia’ as a precursor. Also in WWOT, MacAskill refers to a version of meta-utopia characterised by a progressive (vs conservative) pluralism with the phrase a ‘morally exploratory world’.

❝ Another account which you could interpret as a proposal for viatopia is Robert Nozick’s idea of “meta-utopia” where many different communities pursue different utopian visions, which people are free to leave as they wish, and where no one can impose their utopian vision on others (Anarchy, State and Utopia, p.312). Scott Alexander’s concept of “Archipelago” is similar, as is my concept of a “morally exploratory world”, in What We Owe The Future. In my account, at least, the core idea is that individual free choice would lead to the best societies winning out over time. ❞

– William MacAskill, Viatopia

AGI-Pilled Political Philosophy: Nozick, Rawls, Huntington, Fukuyama, Popper etc

An alternative introduction I wrote was a genealogical account of 20th century political philosophy entering 21st century AI governance & macrostrategy research. Basically I remark on how a number of prominent researchers are updating the ideas of political philosophers for AI: MacAskill is Nozick in the Age of AGI (Viatopia) and Gabriel/Ord are contemporary Rawls’ (Towards a Theory of Justice for AI and ‘the Long Reflection’).

One framing of Mechanics of Meta-Utopia and my other work-in-progress We Need a New Theory of Coordination is that I am revisiting Huntington’s Clash of Civilisations and Fukuyama’s End of History thesis (market capitalism, parliamentary democracy as the best of all possible world-systems) in light of transformative AI.

Another political philosopher that I was remiss to mention in MoMU is Popper: whose open/closed society distinction maps onto the meta-utopian design variable of membrane permeability. In this latter respect, there is an article by the Consilience Project proposing that ‘open societies must evolve with the information revolution’ and ‘coordinate to out-compete closed societies’.

Further afield, entering critical theory Marcuse and Adorno’s One-Dimensional Man & Culture Industry are probably also worth bringing into the conversation on global monoculture that Hanson and MacAskill are having.

Anyways, I think all I wanted to say here is that I think it’s a productive exercise for more people to be troubleshooting theories of how human society should function through the lens of the AI transition. A 2.0 version should also engage more with digital minds, AI cultures and space expansion as pieces like Cleo Nardo’s Stratified Utopia LessWrong (2025), Tegmark’s ‘Libertarian Utopia’ in Life 3.0 (2017), MacAskill’s Afterwards (see The Alliance’s ‘cosmic anarcho-socialism’) and Banks’s A Few Notes on the Culture.

Whilst I say that I am critically revisiting Anarchy, State and Utopia in the subtitle, I barely engaged with the text itself (more so the three themes of the book’s title) and just ran with the basic idea of meta-utopia. For a 2.0 version I’d probably like to engage more with Nozick and other core libertarian ideas (nightwatchman state, non-aggression principle etc) as they relate to ideal post-AGI civilisation design.

Mechanics of Meta-Utopia 2.0?

I think the project could be extended further and made more systematic in a number of ways:

  • AI Capabilities & Civilisation Design: mapping how AI capabilities specifically affect the two variables membrane permeability (physical/kinetic and virtual/memetic borders) and cultural integrity (i.e. socialisation institutions: education, media, church etc).
  • Civilisational Legibility: a third variable on how well a world democratic federation or an AI moral parliament will be able to model the complexity of the meta-utopian system and interactions between sub-topias. In my working doc, I have a separate tab that goes more in-depth on how AGI will affect the three variables.
  • AI Night Watchman: how an AI could serve the function of a stable night watchman state versus a human version that becomes corrupted.
  • The Full Stack Epistemic & Coordination Tech of Meta-Utopia (e.g. including the ‘guardian angels’ and how they interact with the AI night watchman and a world digital democracy etc).
