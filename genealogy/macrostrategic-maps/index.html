Cultural Macrostrategy: Major Frames for the Governance of Cultural Evolution

Here I construct a series of major frames for the (post-AGI) governance of cultural evolution, and conclude by charting future research directions for the frontier macrostrategy field of Coordination Studies.

Cultures & Civilisations: Clash, Conquest, Consolidation, Collapse, Convergence*

* Here I seek to follow the pioneering path of Nathan Sears, who remains the most prominent scholar to put macrostrategy into dialogue with international relations (IR). See International Politics in the Age of Existential Threats (2020), Great Power Rivalry and Macrosecuritization Failure (2022, PhD thesis) and Anarchy, Technology, and the Self-Destruction Hypothesis: Human Survival and the Fermi Paradox (2020), the last of which formalises Bostrom’s paper The Vulnerable World Hypothesis (2019) within IR and SETI (the search for extra-terrestrial intelligence).

❝ World politics is entering a new phase… It is my hypothesis that the fundamental source of conflict in this new world will not be primarily ideological or primarily economic. The great divisions among humankind and the dominating source of conflict will be cultural. Nation states will remain the most powerful actors in world affairs, but the principal conflicts of global politics will occur between nations and groups of different civilizations. The clash of civilizations will dominate global politics. The fault lines between civilizations will be the battle lines of the future. ❞

– Samuel P. Huntington, The Clash of Civilizations? (1993)

The Clash of Civilizations? (1993) is a famous hypothesis by Huntington (an American political scientist) that (post-Cold War) global conflicts in the 21st century will be driven primarily by cultural differences rather than ideological competition or economic conditions.

In contrast to this model of divergent cultural evolution leading to cultural competition and conflict, Bostrom proposes in What is a Singleton? (2005) that ‘a singleton could form… through convergent evolution…’:

❝ e.g. if it turns out that all sufficiently advanced individuals or cultures come to accept fundamentally the same values or goals. These common values in combination with all the individuals and cultures that embrace them would then be an “agency” in the broad sense intended here, and it would constitute a singleton. ❞

This excerpt captures what I will call the Convergence of Civilisations hypothesis. Alongside Clash (Huntington) and Convergence (Bostrom), we can also posit three other future scenarios for how post-AGI cultural dynamics will play out over the long-term future: Conquest, Consolidation and Collapse.

  • The Clash of Civilisations: divergent cultural evolution leading to cultural competition and conflict.
  • The Convergence of Civilisations: convergent cultural evolution leading to cultural cooperation and consensus.
  • The Conquest of Civilisations: offensive cultural dynamics leading to a unilaterally enforced global monoculture.
  • The Consolidation of Civilisations: defensive cultural dynamics leading to a multilaterally enforced global polyculture.
  • The Collapse of Civilisations: stagnated cultural evolution leading to the extinction of cultures and a failure to realise cultural progress.

Based on these cultural attractors we can construct a typology of cultural technologies (AI for culture: epistemics, ontologies, axiologies):

  • Catapults (the memetic equivalent of bunker busters) or memetic ‘Spears’: cyberwarfare technologies for control over population-level psychology (e.g. irregular warfare, foreign propaganda, psyops).
  • Citadels (memetic equivalents of fortress futures) or memetic ‘Shields’: cybersecurity technologies to prevent external influence (e.g. the Golden Shield, the Great Firewall of China) and form hyper-secure subcultures, fragmenting civilisation into a multitude of non-interacting, self-contained small worlds (localised lock-ins).
  • Causeways (the memetic equivalent of physical bridges) or memetic ‘Sanctuaries’: digital democratic communication technologies to facilitate the convergence of multiple cultures into a unified, syncretic world-culture.

Ultimately, the final equilibria of post-AGI cultural dynamics will be determined by which class of technologies becomes more advanced and implemented; in other words, whether civilisation succeeds or fails in steering a process of differential techno-cultural development and deployment.

Differential Cultural Development: Freedom before Fascism

As with technological and intellectual development, in an ideal world cultural development would also be differential: that is, beliefs and values (‘ethico-onto-epistem-ologies’, worldviews, superstructures, memetic complexes etc.) would emerge in a specific sequence, such that cultural evolution as a macrohistorical process would overall be aligned with survival and flourishing.

Differential Cultural Development: trying to retard the proliferation of dangerous ideologies and accelerate the adoption of beneficial worldviews, especially those that ameliorate the hazards posed by ideologies.

Whilst we are not in the idealised position of a meta-utopian civilisation designer (a grander version of the ‘social planner’ in economics) – instead situated within a spontaneous order with limited influence over cultural evolution – we may still have the power to affect the rate of development of various cultures, and potentially the sequence in which cultures are developed and adopted (to what extent depends on how we define “we”).

AI for Culture & Human Value-Alignment: The Transition to Syntopia

Another development within the field of AI alignment and governance is the epistemic turn and the subfield of ‘AI for epistemics’: a growing focus on the foundations of knowledge, belief-formation and truth-seeking in a world increasingly populated and mediated by AI systems, which affect our ability to discern truth, manage large-scale information environments and engage in public discourse.

This turn towards ‘collective epistemics’ (or social epistemology in philosophy) is motivated by concerns that advanced AI will have the capability to manipulate or overwhelm human epistemic processes, potentially leading to ‘epistemic collapse’ or ‘value lock-in’. In other words, AGI will likely be weaponised for mass disinformation and outpace human critical faculties, leading to a loss of agency over our own thought processes and belief/value systems. In this sense, AI for epistemics and coordination can be positioned as a defensive accelerationist (d/acc) counter-measure against cultural existential risks (e.g. value lock-in, misaligned memetic proliferation, ideological fanaticism, epistemic collapse, cyborgregore assimilation).

* FLF is a spin-off from the Future of Life Institute (FLI) focused on ‘AI for human reasoning’:

“The broad, fuzzy domain of potential AI capabilities that can improve the quality of understanding and coordination, more than they make the world complex, we’re calling AI for Human Reasoning. This approach prioritizes developing AI that augments human understanding and decision-making rather than replacing it, ensuring that our collective wisdom can outpace the challenges we create.”

AI for Epistemics: Field-Formation & Literature Map

Most recently Forethought (FTT), in collaboration with the Future of Life Foundation (FLF),* have published a research article, AI Tools for Collective Epistemics (alt. Design Sketches for Collective Epistemics) (2026), following earlier articles What’s Important in “AI for Epistemics”? (August 2024) and Project ideas: Epistemics (January 2024), as well as mentions throughout the institute’s research.

Sourbut, a researcher at FLF, has most recently published on LessWrong AI for Human Reasoning for Rationalists (2026), and previously A Full Epistemic Stack: Knowledge Commons for the 21st Century (2025).

FLF has also publicised the final projects of their flagship fellowship at aiforhumanreasoning.com. At a smaller scale there has also been an AI for Epistemics Hackathon and previously an Essay Competition on the Automation of Wisdom and Philosophy.

Other research in this space includes Building AIs that do human-like philosophy by Carlsmith (2026), Good AI Epistemics as an Offramp from the Intelligence Explosion by Goldhaber (2026, FLF fellowship program director) and ‘AI for societal uplift’ as a path to victory by Douglas (2025).

AI for Epistemics as a framing either implies that thinking is neutral, or has implicit value-judgements. Based on the conceptual engineering of “ethico-onto-epistem-ology” – which points to ‘the inseparability of ethics, ontology and epistemology when engaging in (scientific) knowledge production’ and ‘with the world itself’ – we can extend the field into AI for Epistemics, Ontology and Axiology, covering three foundational domains and questions of philosophy (how we know, what exists, what is of value). In other words, how AI implicates not just belief-formation, but also cognitive mapping and moral psychology.

Further, beyond AI for epistemics, which frames knowledge-production and societal decision-making as an analytic process, we could conceptualise this domain as AI for psychology, even AI for social psychology or, at the grandest level, AI for sociology: how AI can be used to model complex adaptive societal systems and predict population-level behaviour (macrosociological phenomena). Here we are effectively speaking of an actually-existing science of ‘psychohistory’, the fictional meta-discipline imagined by Asimov in the Foundation series (1951–93): ‘a science in which things could be predicted on a probabilistic or statistical basis’.

* A similar but distinct idea is Kulveit’s Sociology of AI (ACS Research):

“We are empirically studying AI ‘psychology’ and ‘sociology’ — investigating the internal states, self-concepts, and social dynamics of large language models… Inter-agent dynamics — examining the social behavior of interacting LLM agents, including persona transfer, cultural dynamics, and emergent cooperation.”

“We are moving beyond the study of individual AI agents toward understanding the complex dynamics of entire ecosystems of intelligence. This involves analyzing interactions between diverse AI systems (such as LLMs), human-AI teams, and existing human institutions. We seek to build a systematic understanding of which properties of intelligent behaviour are universal, convergent, or local across these ecosystems, scales, and substrates. Drawing on traditions including evolutionary biology, cognitive science, statistical physics, economics, ecology, and cybernetics, we are developing a framework for ‘AI Sociology.’”

The ultimate culmination of AI for sociology is the summoning of a Cartesian-Laplacean Demon: a superintelligence capable of controlling what subjects perceive and predicting what subjects will do; manufacturing and anticipating human behaviour.* Descartes’s Demon and Laplace’s Demon are two classical Enlightenment thought experiments from the history of philosophy that can be combined and reinterpreted as a technological prophecy for superintelligence (if you are willing to think like an applied interdisciplinary philosopher ;).

Creating a Cartesian-Laplacean Angel: Aligning Perceptual & Predictive Power

The moral imperative for steerers of cultural evolution and human alignment researchers is to wisely use this newfound techno-science to realise a Cartesian-Laplacean Angel: an aligned superintelligence that uses its complete technology of human behaviour to steer humans, potentially over intergenerational time, towards the most moral world-culture, and ultimately realise moral progress in a way that is subjectively perceived as voluntary and beneficial by the members of current cultures in the world today.

* Transition risk is a conceptual engineering introduced by Ord (The Precipice, 2020):

“Transition risks: risks that arise during a transition to a new technological or social regime. For example the risks as we develop and deploy transformative AGI are like this, as are the risks of climate change as we transition to being a high-energy civilization. Rushing the transition may do nothing to lower these risks—indeed it could easily heighten them. But if the transition is necessary or highly desirable, we may have to go through it at some point, so mere delay is not a solution, and may also make things worse. The general prescription for these risks is neither haste nor slowness, but care and foresight.”

This Cartesian-Laplacean Angel would enact a reflective process of coherent blended volition (CBV) to – safely, wisely and benevolently – navigate the transition (risks)* between the intermediate stage of Poly-Utopia (plurality of utopias) and the ideal civilisational culture that I call Syntopia (synthesis of utopias).

Coherent blended volition (CBV) “proposes a creative blending process that produces new, harmonious value systems that all participants would recognize as adequately representing their contributions.” (AI Safety Atlas, 2025)

Syntopia

Syntopia is an ideal civilisational end-state in which humanity has converged on a universal morality, ‘perennial philosophy’ and syncretic culture. All minds share in the truth (epistemics), goodness (ethics) and beauty (aesthetics) of a grand synthesis, which transcends and includes all worldviews from history in an emergent whole, aligned with the nature of interobjective reality and the conditions of existential flourishing. Life is lived pluralistically and dynamically, without locking in the future to a singular way of life or form of being, but instead oscillating between cultures across (what would for humans be multiple) lifetimes. Syntopia could potentially emerge from mass multi-directional migration and cultural cross-pollination over intergenerational time, or from AI-enhanced epistemics at a civilisational scale.

Syntopia is similar to the cultural stage of metamodernism, or Teal civilisation in integral theory:

Metamodernism – the culmination of the historical sequence of cultural logics, from traditional religions (Pre-modern) and Enlightenment reason (Modern) to sceptical deconstruction (Postmodern) to a synthesis of them all (Metamodern).

Integral Theory – the development of human consciousness and social structures through stages of increasing interior and exterior complexity toward a final stage (colour-coded ‘teal’ in spiral dynamics) that integrates all previous worldviews. Each stage transcends and includes the ones before it, expanding the collective capacity for empathy, logic and perspective-taking.

To reach Syntopia requires facing what I have previously diagnosed as The Dark Side of Meta-Utopia, and what MacAskill in What We Owe the Future calls the lock-in paradox:

❝ This… point—that we need to structure global society so that cultural evolution guides the world towards better values and better societal structures—highlights an issue facing the design of a morally exploratory world that I’ll call the lock-in paradox. We need to lock in some institutions and ideas in order to prevent a more thoroughgoing lock-in of values. One challenge is that these institutions and ideas will be morally controversial; for example, from many fundamentalist religious perspectives, the idea that we would encourage or even allow a diversity of worldviews might be regarded as abominable. Similarly, the idea that the path to the correct moral view is via reflection and good-faith debate, rather than studying the scripture of a holy book, is not one that everyone would accept. The lock-in paradox thus resembles the familiar paradox of tolerance—the necessity for liberal societies to defend themselves against intolerant views that would undermine their freedom, even if doing so requires curtailing the very tolerance they want to preserve. ❞

– William MacAskill, What We Owe the Future (2022)

Preparing for the Cultural Explosion: Hyper-Acceleration & Differentiation

❝ Explosions are manifestly dangerous. from any perspective that is really (which is to say historically) instantiated. Only in the most radically anomalous cases can they be durably sustained. It is the firm prediction of accelerationism, therefore, that the typical practical topic of modern civilization will be the controlled explosion, commonly translated as governance. or regulation. ❞

– Teleoplexy: Notes on Acceleration by Nick Land (2014)

Forethought have recently argued that civilisation must begin Preparing for the Intelligence Explosion, and have further extended this problem-framing to incorporate what they term The Industrial Explosion, of physical capabilities taking off alongside cognitive ones.

Beyond digital software and hardware, I propose that we also need to prepare for the Cultural Explosion: the rapid acceleration of cultural production, differentiation and propagation enabled by the capabilities of AGI.

This phenomenon has previously been discussed in relation to Misaligned Culture and Gradual Disempowerment, with an emphasis on the acceleration of cultural evolution and AI-AI culture expanding and out-competing legacy human culture in the noosphere:

❝ Beyond shifting what kinds of cultural variants are selected for, AI systems could dramatically accelerate the pace of cultural evolution itself. This acceleration presents distinct risks, even if selection pressures remain human-centric. With vastly more computational power applied to generating and testing cultural variants, we might see: More effective exploitation of human cognitive biases; More extreme ideological variants; Faster erosion of equilibria that previously helped maintain social stability; Reduced time for humans to develop cultural “antibodies” against harmful patterns…

With gradual increases in the capabilities and autonomy of AI systems, we may even expect a growing share of communication between AIs, and AIs participating in culture essentially independently. Instead of augmenting human cultural participation, they might start to replace key components. ❞

– Misaligned Culture in Gradual Disempowerment, Jan Kulveit et al., Alignment of Complex Systems Research Group (2025)

The Cultural Communication Problem: Computational Complexity

In a forthcoming (2027) book, Law, Liberty & Leviathan, Anders Sandberg (Chapter 6: The Rationality of Planned Economies: Is the Classic Critique of Socialism Undone by AI?) revisits the economic calculation problem in the light of superintelligence.

* I have written about the emerging discourse on markets, superintelligence and the economic calculation problem at Hayek’s Great Wall: Under Siege in the Age of AI?

Classically, market economists in the Austrian tradition contend that the computational complexity of the economy exceeds the cognitive capabilities of any centralised planner to rationally allocate resources. The argument from superintelligence posits that with the advent of ASI there will finally be an agent fit for this task, and hence the market becomes obsolete as a mechanism of economic calculation and resource allocation. The objection is that computational complexity is not agent-relative, but is inherent to reality and therefore beyond the capabilities of any possible calculator, even a superintelligent one.

Here I propose there is a parallel to this problem from political economy within (post-AGI) cultural epistemology that I call the Cultural Communication Problem: agents cannot efficiently model or predict the minds and behaviour of other agents due to extreme cultural differentiation, divergence and defensiveness.

  • Differentiation: creating a diversity of alien subcultures and collectives of AI minds.
  • Divergence: cultures rapidly move away from any shared common ground, making their core values and assumptions mutually incomprehensible and incompatible.
  • Defensiveness: cultures adopting isolationist, protective measures (e.g. memetic ‘firewalls’) to rigidly safeguard their internal coherence against external influence or assimilation.

This poses the risk of civilisation vectoring toward the Global Polyculture attractor, where the failure of cross-cultural communication prevents the necessary mechanisms of cultural selection and synthesis from operating on the massive cultural variation generated by the AI-accelerated cultural evolutionary process. Even if benevolent cultural forms exist, they cannot effectively spread or cohere into a shared global culture if the computational overhead of translating, integrating and achieving consensus across hyper-differentiated groups is too high. This ultimately threatens collective epistemics, moral progress and civilisation-wide coordination.
